### Playback

- Tracks switch faster when their opening audio is already on the listener’s device. While the current song has enough audio buffered, light-ear fetches up to 2 MiB from each of the next two queue entries, one at a time. Playback and seeking take priority, and the room still waits for a majority to be ready before starting together.
- Fully buffered songs can now play through their final seconds without repeated “buffer quorum lost” pauses. This also fixes a stall when Bilibili reports a duration slightly longer than the decoded audio.

### Reconnection

- If the peer connecting two groups leaves, the remaining clients try direct connections and can reconnect through a known relay. Recovery works for room members and for people still browsing rooms. A relay with a reachable reservation is sufficient even when only one side can receive incoming connections.

### Desktop

- The taskbar or Dock shows the current song’s progress, including pauses and seeks. A new chat received while the window is minimized adds a dot on Windows or an unread count on macOS and supported Linux launchers. Returning to the window clears the hint. Messages loaded from chat history stay quiet.

Choose the desktop installer for your platform, or use **Install and restart** when prompted. The separate `light-ear-relay-*` downloads are for relay hosts. [Relay setup](https://github.com/switefaster/light-ear/blob/v0.2.2/README.md#run)

Desktop builds include the Fraunhofer FDK AAC decoder. Its license notice ships with the release assets. The decoder source is in the archive’s `aac/` directory. [Decoder source](https://static.crates.io/crates/fdk-aac-sys/fdk-aac-sys-0.5.0.crate)
